01 | Core Idea

Not another ESP32 breakout board

The platform should sit between a trainer kit and a professional prototyping bench: students learn the circuit, but do not spend most of the laboratory period fighting with duplicated VCC/GND/signal jumper wiring.

01

Universal carrier

ESP32 socket, protected GPIO, ADC, DAC, I²C, SPI, UART, PWM, level shifting, power rails and test points.

02

Functional modules

Sensors, relays, motors, displays, RFID, RS485, CAN, LoRa and other modules connect through standardized interfaces.

03

Engineering lab

Students can move from GPIO and IoT to power conversion, signal conditioning, current/voltage measurement and industrial communication.

Design principle
Make routine connections standardized, while deliberately leaving selected test points, headers and a small prototyping area so students can still learn real wiring, debugging and circuit construction.
01A | V1 Design Decision

Start small. Solve the wiring problem first.

The first product should not be a large trainer. It should be a compact ESP32 Universal Companion Board whose primary job is to make routine student experiments plug-and-play. Everything else is added only when the curriculum needs it.

V1 = Minimal Viable Platform
01

ESP32 stays familiar

Use the replaceable 38-pin ESP32 DevKit. Students still see, program and debug the actual controller.

02

Standard connectors

6 × 3-pin GPIO/IO ports, 2 × I²C ports and 2 × servo ports replace repetitive VCC/GND/signal wiring.

03

Small prototyping zone

Keep a compact breadboard/header area so jumper wires remain available where learning the circuit is the objective.

04

Add modules later

Power, actuator, instrumentation and industrial modules are separate purchases, not forced into V1.

03 | Power Electronics + Instrumentation

This is the major differentiator

The proposed platform should not stop at sensors and GPIO. It should let E&C students observe, measure and manipulate the power and analog side of an IoT system.

⚡

DC power tree

  • • USB-C 5V input
  • • 7–24V DC external input
  • • regulated 5V and 3.3V rails
  • • protected adjustable AUX rail
  • • separate motor/servo supply
↕

Converter lab

  • • adjustable buck
  • • boost
  • • buck-boost
  • • fixed 5V / 3.3V regulators
  • • enable, current limit and test points
📏

Measurement lab

  • • voltage measurement
  • • high-side current sensing
  • • protected ADC inputs
  • • external DAC output
  • • optional 4–20 mA interface
Safety architecture
Do not expose 230V AC terminals on the student base board. Use a certified enclosed AC-DC adapter, or a separate supervised mains trainer.

06 | Wiring Philosophy

Minimize wires, don't eliminate learning

Use the interactive comparison below.

Wires should normally NOT be needed for

  • ✓ BME280 / DHT / PIR / basic I²C sensors
  • ✓ OLED / RTC / I²C ADC/DAC modules
  • ✓ SG90/MG90S servo ports
  • ✓ onboard relay channels
  • ✓ onboard buttons, LEDs, buzzer and potentiometer
  • ✓ standard RS485/CAN expansion modules

Wires SHOULD still be available for

  • • breadboard and free-form circuit experiments
  • • oscilloscope / logic-analyzer probing and test leads
  • • external DC motors, pumps, solenoids and custom loads
  • • custom transistor/MOSFET/op-amp circuits
  • • external 4–20 mA loops and industrial sensors
  • • non-standard third-party modules and student-designed circuits

08 | Recommended Development Roadmap

Build it in the right order

PHASE 01

Freeze interfaces

Choose ESP32 form factor, connector family, pin map, power rails and module mechanical standard.

PHASE 02

Prototype carrier

Build ESP32 + I/O + power + measurement first. Validate protection and connector ergonomics before adding everything.

PHASE 03

Module ecosystem

Create 10–20 standardized sensor/actuator modules with identical labeling, pin conventions and documentation.

PHASE 04

Curriculum + scale

Map modules to experiments, lab sheets, firmware examples and bulk procurement. Then optimize the BOM for 50/100/250 kits.

Final recommendation

Do not make a giant single PCB containing every sensor. Make a durable universal carrier and a standardized family of inexpensive plug-in modules. Integrate the high-value infrastructure: power, protection, measurement, connectors, actuator drivers and test points. Keep the replaceable ESP32 and most sensors/actuators modular.

Procurement Note

Existing products are references, not the final answer

The market already proves demand for plug-and-play ESP32 trainers and broad IoT experiment boards. The opportunity is to combine that usability with an E&C-oriented power and instrumentation layer, while keeping the platform affordable enough for institutional deployment.
